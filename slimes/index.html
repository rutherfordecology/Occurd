Slime Moulds Near Me v1.1

What it does

It asks iNaturalist which slime moulds have been recorded near you in the last 20 years — within 50 km unless you change it — then sorts them by how often they turn up, so the top of the list is your best bet. Choosing in season now narrows that to what is usually found this month and next; choosing any time of year keeps everything.

"Slime mould" here means the whole of the Mycetozoa: the true, plasmodial slime moulds (Myxomycetes) that make up nearly all the records, plus the coral slimes (Ceratiomyxa) and the rarely-seen cellular and protostelid slime moulds. It does not include the water moulds or the "slime moulds" of lawns that are really fungi.

Why "Needs ID" is on

Slime moulds are hard to get confirmed. Many can only be named for certain under a microscope, so in New Zealand fewer than a third of the records on iNaturalist reach Research Grade. Counting only those leaves most places with a handful of species, so this app counts the "Needs ID" observations too. Untick it in settings if you want only the confirmed ones.

How "in season" is worked out

Fruiting bodies, the stage people notice and photograph, come and go with the weather. For each species on your list the app looks at every record within 300 km, whatever the year, and asks what share of them fell in this month or the next. Two months out of twelve is a sixth; a species with at least that share is in season, one with half as much again is at its peak, and one with under half its fair share is out of season and left off the in-season list. With fewer than eight records to go on it says season unknown rather than guess, and keeps it on both lists.

This is a record of when people found them, not a lab measurement of when they fruit. Records follow observers as well as slime moulds: wet weekends, summer holidays and organised forays all bunch them up. Open any card to see the month by month counts it is based on.

Slime conditions (New Zealand only)

Above the list, the app rates the recent weather as good, average or poor for finding slime moulds. It comes from comparing the weather before about 6,700 New Zealand slime mould records with the weather before plant records taken in the same months, which cancels out the days people simply happened to be out walking. No observer counts more than 50 records, so one keen recorder can't tilt it.

What showed up: everywhere, a humid past week (more humid than usual for the place and time of year) is the main sign. In the North Island a warmer than usual fortnight helps too; in the South Island, a wetter than usual spell one to two weeks earlier. Records turn up roughly one and a half to two times as often in good conditions as in poor ones. That is a nudge, not a forecast — a damp gully can be full of them in a dry week.

The weather is NASA POWER's daily data for the roughly 50 km grid square you are in, which runs a few days behind, so the rating says which date it is up to. Only the grid square is sent to NASA, never your position. Outside New Zealand the rating is left off, because the model was only fitted to New Zealand records.

Crowded places

When a search returns more than 25 species the app pulls the radius in until it doesn't, stopping at 1 km. It tells you when it has done this, and you can override it in one tap. If even 1 km is crowded, the list pages 25 at a time.

Obscured records

iNaturalist publishes a randomised position for threatened and sensitive species, and for any observation whose observer chose to hide its location, scattered by up to roughly 25 km. The obscured option in settings runs a second 50 km sweep just for those and lists them at the bottom, marked with a dashed border.

Photos

Photographs come from iNaturalist and are shown only where the photographer has applied a Creative Commons licence. Each one is credited, and tapping any photo opens it large.

Your location

Your position never leaves your device un-rounded — it is snapped to roughly 100 m before any search is sent, which makes no difference across a 50 km radius. It is not stored, and it never appears in the address bar.

The usual 50 km sits mid-slider, so you can go either way from it. Steps get finer as you go smaller, to pin down a single reserve or gully, and coarser as you go bigger, to sweep a whole region.

At 50 km and below, a search that turns up more than 25 species is narrowed automatically until the list is usable. Set it above 50 km and it is left exactly where you put it.
